THE COLLECTION

The Collection is one body: seven finished compositions, each embodied in its master phonogram — 18 minutes, 41 seconds of R&B and neo-soul by Byron Jupiter, recorded at The Box Studios in New Orleans. The pieces are on view below, each with its placard. Every composition in the Collection is available to license, and every phonogram also works as a score.

Nº 01

Heartfelt (so-so kicks)

Soul worn openly, drums kept humble — a composition that leads with its heart and stays out of the singer's way.

As a score: the letter finally read aloud; reconciliation; sincerity that has to feel true.

Nº 02

What if

The question every great hook asks — built for the verse that wonders and the chorus that answers.

As a score: the crossroads cue; second thoughts; the road not taken.

Nº 03

Coffee

Morning-after warmth with a slow burn — smooth enough for the slow jam, awake enough to move.

As a score: first light, kitchens and cafés; the rituals that carry a relationship.

Nº 04

Hey What About You

A call across the room that turns into a conversation — playful bounce with room for two voices.

As a score: the reunion, the unexpected call; two people finding the thread again.

Nº 05

Stoop to

Porch-level soul from New Orleans — low, close, and unhurried, with the neighborhood in its pocket.

As a score: the block, the porch, coming home; a sense-of-place cue.

Nº 06

Plenty of Phases 2

One relationship, every season of it — a composition that changes its weather and keeps its soul.

As a score: time passing, chapters turning; montage and series arcs.

Nº 07

Dead Silence

What isn't said, said loudest — sparse, heavy space for the vocal that carries the weight alone.

As a score: the pause after the news; aftermath; grief held quiet.

WHAT THE WORDS MEAN

This page uses exact words, and exact words deserve plain teaching. If this is your first license, start here — the words below are the whole system. And remember where you are: this is an exhibition. The Collection is on view; a guest who wishes to take a work into a record or a picture does so by license.

Composition — the underlying musical work: the music itself — its melody, harmony, rhythm, and structure — as written. A composition exists before any recording of it. On the publishing side, each composition here is written by Byron Jupiter (100%) and published by Good Delivery (BMI).

Arrangement — the composition as Byron Jupiter voiced and ordered it: the instrumentation, the voicings, the structure in time — the form in which this desk issues the work. Arrangement by Byron Jupiter. A buyer licenses a composition in Byron Jupiter's arrangement, embodied in its phonogram.

Phonogram — the fixation of the sounds of a performance: the recorded master, the ℗ side of the work. When you listen to anything on this page, you are hearing a phonogram. Each composition here is embodied in one master phonogram, recorded at The Box Studios, New Orleans.

License — written permission to use a composition — in Byron Jupiter's arrangement — and its phonogram, for a named use, at a named scope, for a named term. A license is not a sale of the work: ownership stays with the house. The buyer receives the work as delivered, for the use agreed, with the terms in writing before delivery.

Use — what the work is doing, never what the work is. The same composition may be used as a beat under a record — an artist's song built on it — or as a score under picture, in film, television, and media. “Beat” and “score” name uses. The work itself is a composition; its master is a phonogram.

USES

A license names a use. Below is the whole map: every lane a composition and its phonogram can travel, gathered in seven classes. Each use rides its own written instrument, quoted by scope — a named use, at a named scope, for a named term. Nothing is granted by conversation, by preview, or by silence in a document. Name the use you mean; the desk answers with the paper it rides.

Recordings — the work fixed in a new record, or the phonogram itself reused: an artist records a new song over the phonogram; the master is placed as-is; a portion is sampled, or re-performed as an interpolation; another singer covers the composition; the phonogram is remixed, or re-recorded for karaoke. The flagship lane for artists, and the lane where the phonogram's own identity is licensed, never sold.

Picture — the work fixed against images: feature film, television and streaming series, documentary, trailers and promos, advertising and brand films, video games, animation, and a brand's own short-form video. In every picture use, two grants travel together — the composition, in Byron Jupiter's arrangement, and its phonogram — and the fee is quoted by scope: media, term, territory, and placement named per use.

Performance — the work heard by an audience, live or transmitted: concerts and paid shows, DJ and club play, radio in all its forms, streaming, background in shops and rooms, worship, schools and ensembles, and theater and dance. Most of this class is collected through society and statutory schemes rather than granted at the desk — the desk's work there is registration and record — and grand rights in theater and dance are always a direct written negotiation.

Words & Print — the work on the page: sheet music and printed editions, the lyrics reprinted in books, liner notes, and articles, and the lyrics displayed inside music apps. Print is the composition's oldest use, and in every form it carries the writer's and publisher's names on its face.

Digital & New — the work in software, feeds, and machines: podcasts, audiobooks, apps, ringtones, creator-platform libraries, virtual worlds and virtual performance, and wellness, sleep, and functional listening. Every digital use is named in writing; silence in an instrument is never a grant.

Reserved — AI training and machine-learning uses: rights reserved — never granted by any tier, only by an explicit written instrument.

Institutional — the buyer is an institution, and the purchase runs on the institution's paper: schools, universities, and districts; museums and exhibitions; libraries and archives; hospitals and care systems; government and civic bodies; churches; and companies licensing for their own programs. Site licenses and term licenses, quoted by scope, with bookkeeping-grade documentation and invoicing, because institutions buy on paper.

Supervision — the meta-use: not one work licensed, but the desk's ear over many. A production brings its picture; sound and mood are selected from the Collection and beyond it, placed, timed, and cleared in writing. The service is its own agreement; the licenses for the works selected ride their own instruments.

Use ≠ identity — a use names what the work is doing, never what the work is. The same composition may stand under a record, under picture, in a room, or on a page; it remains a composition in Byron Jupiter's arrangement, embodied in its phonogram.

What a music supervisor gets: broadcast-ready WAV of the phonogram as delivered; stems, instrumental edits, and timing versions where the placement requires them, agreed in the written license; a clean rights picture — writer Byron Jupiter (100%), publisher Good Delivery (BMI), with the works' license documentation held in the house registry and produced on request. Fees are quoted by scope, in writing — no rate card, no surprises after the fact.

MUSIC SUPERVISION

Beside licensing from the Collection, this desk offers music supervision as a packaged service. A production brings its picture — a scene, a sequence, a spot — and Byron Jupiter supervises the music: sound & mood selections drawn from the Collection and beyond it, placed, timed, and cleared in writing.

Sound & Mood — selections chosen for what the scene says and what the audience is meant to feel: the sound under the moment, the mood the moment carries. Selections begin in the Collection and reach beyond it where the picture asks for more.

Placed & Timed — every selection is placed against the picture as delivered and timed to it, so the music arrives where the scene needs it and leaves when the scene is done.

Cleared in Writing — every selection is cleared in writing before delivery. On Collection works the rights picture is one line — writer Byron Jupiter (100%), publisher Good Delivery (BMI); selections from beyond the Collection are documented the same way, in the house registry.

The Service — your production is honored for arriving at this desk. Music supervision is a packaged service, quoted by scope, agreed in writing before work begins. Contract with Byron Jupiter; write the door below and name the picture, the scenes, and the scope.
